\section{Introduction}
Simulating realistic multi-agent crowd behaviors is essential for evaluating autonomous vehicles and service robots in urban settings. Classical microscopic pedestrian models, such as the Social Force Model~\cite{helbing1995social} and Optimal Reciprocal Collision Avoidance (ORCA)~\cite{vandenberg2011reciprocal}, rely on explicit physical and geometric heuristics. While computationally efficient, they produce rigid, uniform maneuvers that struggle to capture the behavioral diversity, subtle concessions, and multimodality of real crowds.

Recent approaches turn to deep generative modeling, such as continuous Flow Matching~\cite{lipman2023flow} in CrowdES~\cite{bae2025crowdes}, learning continuous velocity fields from demonstration data (e.g., ETH/UCY datasets). Despite their expressiveness, deploying deep generative models directly in closed-loop interactive simulations reveals two key structural limitations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*), labelsep=0.5em, noitemsep, topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Chunked Decision Latency:} Simulators generate paths in open-loop temporal chunks ($T_f = 2\,\text{s}$ at $5\,\text{fps}$) governed by modes predicted once per chunk. An agent operating in this window cannot react to dynamic changes in the surrounding crowd, causing delayed and unnatural frozen states.
    \item \textbf{Black-Box Opacity:} End-to-end neural decoders map latent states directly into future coordinates without symbolic logic, preventing developers from auditing why an agent chose to yield, swerve, or stop.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve decision latency and black-box opacity while preserving generative realism, we propose \textbf{Flow2BT}, a framework that distills continuous generative crowd policies into interpretable \textit{Behavior Trees} (BT)~\cite{colledanchise2018behavior}. Motivated by tree-flow duality~\cite{ramachandran2026trees}, our architecture treats the generative flow model as an expressive macro-teacher: we extract topological bifurcations in a NavMesh-aligned frame, induce linear condition hyperplanes, and compress unimodal branches into 2nd-order Dynamical Movement Primitives (DMPs)~\cite{chatzilygeroudis2021behavior}. Ticking at $100\,\text{Hz}$, the tree enables a $10\,\text{ms}$ control update cycle with preemption via Fallback control nodes upon environmental changes.
\newpage
Our key contributions and findings are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Neuro-Symbolic Distillation:} We show that continuous crowd policies can be distilled into interpretable Behavior Trees with closed-form DMP leaves, enabling a $100\,\text{Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 10\,\text{ms}$) reactive evaluation rate compared to $2000\,\text{ms}$ open-loop chunking.
    \item \textbf{Generative Realism Preservation:} We demonstrate that Flow2BT approximately preserves macroscopic spatial density and dynamic population distributions while providing interpretable symbolic execution, despite an inherent microscopic kinematic trade-off.
    \item \textbf{Reactive Locomotion Primitives:} We parameterize unimodal action leaves into closed-form Dynamical Movement Primitives (DMPs), maintaining smooth navigation without open-loop chunk latency.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Generative Pedestrian Locomotion}
Pedestrian simulation has progressed from rule-based models like SFM~\cite{helbing1995social} and ORCA~\cite{vandenberg2011reciprocal} to expressive data-driven architectures~\cite{alahi2016social,lipman2023flow}. CrowdES~\cite{bae2025crowdes} achieves continuous crowd generation conditioned on NavMesh waypoints. However, existing generative simulators rely on open-loop chunking ($T_f = 2\,\text{s}$ at $5\,\text{fps}$), sampling modes ($B=8$) only once per chunk. In dense crowds, an agent committed to this horizon cannot react to dynamic movements of neighbors, causing rigid trajectories and frozen deadlocks.

\subsection{Behavior Trees and Robot Control}
Behavior Trees (BTs) provide modular, reactive robot execution via Sequence ($\to$) and Fallback ($?$) nodes~\cite{colledanchise2018behavior,iovino2022survey}, supporting transparent auditing and preemption. However, synthesizing BTs via genetic programming~\cite{iovino2022survey} suffers from tree bloat, while differentiable relaxations~\cite{huang2025differentiable} face severe discretization gaps upon deployment. 
Trajectory-based BTs and Dynamical Movement Primitives (DMPs)~\cite{chatzilygeroudis2021behavior,ijspeert2013dynamical} bridge continuous execution with symbolic switching. Recently, Ramachandran and Sra~\cite{ramachandran2026trees} showed that continuous velocity fields naturally coarse-grain into hierarchical dendrograms under reverse time. Flow2BT builds on this duality, using flow policies as teachers to induce interpretable BT topologies without grammar search.

\section{Proposed Method}
As illustrated in \Fig{fig:overview}, the proposed Flow2BT architecture systematically distills a high-capacity continuous generative crowd policy into an interpretable, reactive Behavior Tree. The pipeline is structured into three interconnected operational stages:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*), labelsep=0.5em]
    \item \textbf{Teacher Flow-Based Policy Training:} Learning continuous locomotive vector fields conditioned on global NavMesh tracking and ego-centric multi-agent context.
    \item \textbf{Flow-to-Tree Topology Discovery:} Rolling out continuous trajectory ensembles and extracting a hierarchical decision dendrogram via reverse-time coarse-graining.
    \item \textbf{Tree-to-Reactive BT Assembly:} Inducing maximum-margin condition guards, parameterizing action leaves into 2nd-order DMPs, and assembling a $100\,\text{Hz}$ reactive Behavior Tree with closed-form action execution.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Teacher Flow-Based Policy Training}
\textbf{NavMesh Guidance and Geometry:} 
Each pedestrian agent $i$ has dynamic state $\mathbf{s}_i = [\mathbf{p}_i^\top, \mathbf{v}_i^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^4$, where $\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ is position and $\mathbf{v}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ is velocity. Following CrowdES~\cite{bae2025crowdes}, the walkable scene geometry is discretized into a convex polygonal navigation mesh (NavMesh) using the Recast navigation toolset~\cite{mononen2009recast}. Rather than repeatedly computing full global paths, we initialize global $A^*$ search once at agent spawn and project current agent coordinates onto the cached polyline at runtime, reducing pathfinding queries by over $600\times$ while serving local lookahead waypoints $\mathbf{c}_{i, \text{nav}} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (\Fig{fig:overview}, left).

\textbf{Ego-Centric Perceptual Feature ($\phi(\mathbf{s})$):}
To capture immediate geometric affordances and dynamic pedestrian interactions, each agent constructs an ego-centric feature vector $\phi(\mathbf{s}_i) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ matching the sensory schema in \Fig{fig:overview}:
\begin{equation}
    \phi(\mathbf{s}_i) = \begin{bmatrix}
    d_{\text{obstacle}} \\
    \mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}} \\
    \text{TTC} \\
    \theta_{\text{nav}} \\
    d_{\text{clearance}}
    \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
    \|\mathbf{p}_{\text{obs}} - \mathbf{p}_i\| \\
    \mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_i \\
    \|\mathbf{p}_j - \mathbf{p}_i\| / (\|\mathbf{v}_j - \mathbf{v}_i\| + \epsilon) \\
    \angle(\mathbf{c}_{i, \text{nav}} - \mathbf{p}_i) - \angle(\mathbf{v}_i) \\
    \min(d_{\text{left}}, d_{\text{right}})
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\text{obstacle}} \in \mathbb{R}$ is distance to the nearest static boundary (m), $j$ indexes the nearest dynamic pedestrian, $\text{TTC}$ is estimated time-to-collision (s) stabilized with $\epsilon = 10^{-4}\,\text{m/s}$ for static or co-moving walkers, $\theta_{\text{nav}}$ is heading deviation from the NavMesh waypoint (rad), and $d_{\text{clearance}}$ denotes lateral corridor boundaries (m).

\textbf{Continuous Locomotion Flow Matching Policy:}
Rather than using discrete Markov state jumps or stochastic diffusion steps, the macro-teacher is formulated as a continuous velocity field $v_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, \mathbf{C}_s)$ trained via Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)~\cite{lipman2023flow}. Let $p_1$ denote the empirical target distribution of pedestrian footstep displacements $\mathbf{x}_1 = \mathbf{v}_{\text{demo}} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ ($\text{m/s}$) conditioned on context $\mathbf{C}_s = [\phi(\mathbf{s}_i), \mathbf{c}_{i, \text{nav}} - \mathbf{p}_i]$, and let $p_0 = \mathcal{N}(0, \mathbf{I})$ be the standard Gaussian prior. We define the conditional optimal transport (OT) probability path:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_t = (1 - t)\mathbf{x}_0 + t \mathbf{x}_1, \quad \dot{\mathbf{x}}_t = \mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0, \quad t \in [0, 1]
\end{equation}
The neural vector field $v_\theta$ is parameterized by a 4-layer MLP with hidden dimensions $[256, 512, 512, 256]$ and $128$-dimensional sinusoidal temporal embeddings. The conditioning vector $\mathbf{C}_s$ is embedded via the CrowdES context encoder (latent dimension 256, hidden dimension 2048) over an 8-frame observation history ($1.6\,\text{s}$). The network is trained with the AdamW optimizer (learning rate $10^{-4}$) on the standard ETH training split by minimizing the mean squared flow regression loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, \mathbf{x}_0, \mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{C}_s} \left[ \left\| v_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t, \mathbf{C}_s) - (\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0) \right\|_2^2 \right]
\end{equation}
The resulting straight vector field eliminates curvature artifacts, generating high-fidelity multimodal velocities that capture natural crowd concessions and yielding maneuvers.

\subsection{Flow-to-Tree Topology Discovery}
\textbf{Ensemble Trajectory Rollouts:}
With the trained continuous flow policy acting as a generative teacher, we roll out an offline ensemble of $M$ trajectory rollouts across diverse initial states $\mathbf{s}_0 \sim \mathcal{S}_0$:
\begin{equation}
    \Xi = \{\xi_k(\tau)\}_{k=1}^M, \quad \frac{d\xi_k}{d\tau} = v_\theta(\xi_k(\tau), \tau, \mathbf{C}_s^{(k)}), \quad \tau \in [0, T_f]
\end{equation}
integrating the ODE over horizon $T_f = 2.0\,\text{s}$ ($\Delta t = 0.2\,\text{s}$, 10 future steps) using an adaptive Dormand-Prince solver. We sample $S = 1,024$ unique context states with $R = 16$ stochastic rollouts per state ($1024 \times 16$, yielding $M = 16,384$ paths) to capture multimodal branching.

\textbf{Reverse-Time Flow Coarse-Graining \& Dendrogram Extraction:}
Following tree-flow duality~\cite{ramachandran2026trees}, tracing continuous streamlines backward in time ($\tau = T_f - t$) coarsens the multimodal distribution, naturally grouping distinct trajectories into hierarchical clusters via pairwise spectral distance:
\begin{align}
    D(\xi_i, \xi_j) &= \int_0^{T_f} \|\xi_i(t) - \xi_j(t)\|_2^2 \, dt + \lambda_{\text{term}} \|\Delta \xi_{ij}(T_f)\|_2^2 \nonumber \\
    &\quad + \lambda_{\text{vel}} \int_0^{T_f} \|\dot{\xi}_i(t) - \dot{\xi}_j(t)\|_2^2 \, dt
\end{align}
where $\Delta \xi_{ij}(T_f) \triangleq \xi_i(T_f) - \xi_j(T_f)$ denotes the terminal displacement offset. Ward's hierarchical clustering constructs binary dendrogram $\mathcal{T}_{\text{topo}}$ (\Fig{fig:overview}, middle). Bifurcation nodes $k$ are detected when variance exceeds threshold $\epsilon_{\text{split}}$, partitioning trajectories into $B=8$ unimodal leaf bundles $\Xi_\ell$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Tree-to-Reactive BT}{Tree to Reactive BT} Assembly}
\textbf{Condition Node Induction via Soft-Margin SVM:}
At each bifurcation node $k \in \mathcal{T}_{\text{topo}}$, the child clusters $\Xi_{\text{left}}^{(k)}$ and $\Xi_{\text{right}}^{(k)}$ define binary targets $y \in \{+1, -1\}$. Using ego-centric features $\phi(\mathbf{s})$ collected at encounter onsets, we fit a soft-margin SVM (\Fig{fig:overview}, right):
\begin{align}
    \min_{\mathbf{w}_k, b_k, \boldsymbol{\xi}} \ & \frac{1}{2} \|\mathbf{w}_k\|_2^2 + C \sum_{m=1}^{M_k} \xi_m \\
    \text{s.t.} \ & y_m (\mathbf{w}_k^\top \phi(\mathbf{s}_{i, m}) + b_k) \ge 1 - \xi_m, \quad \xi_m \ge 0 \nonumber
\end{align}
During runtime execution, the condition predicate is evaluated in $< 1\,\mu\text{s}$:
\begin{equation}
    C_k(\mathbf{s}) = \begin{cases} \text{SUCCESS} & \text{if } \mathbf{w}_k^\top \phi(\mathbf{s}) + b_k \ge 0 \\ \text{FAILURE} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In the NavMesh frame, cross-validated classification accuracy reaches $84.4\% - 91.2\%$, achieving an end-to-end tree routing fidelity of $71.9\%$. Crucially, the resulting tree provides human-auditable decision predicates. For example, the root bifurcation guard evaluates a linear hyperplane over speed and neighbor clearance (accuracy $92.7\%$): $-2.12 v_i - 0.358 d_{\text{nav}} + 0.091 v_{\text{close}} + 1.79 \ge 0$, separating slow or yielding pedestrians from direct locomotion. Subsequent guards evaluate heading deviation $\theta_{\text{nav}}$ (weights $+1.34$ and $+1.62$) to route agents into lateral swerve primitives (\texttt{swerve\_left\_3}, \texttt{swerve\_right\_6}) versus forward marching (\texttt{march\_2}, \texttt{march\_7}), rendering the decision logic fully inspectable.

\textbf{Action Leaf Parameterization via 2nd-Order DMPs:}
Each unimodal leaf cluster $\ell \in \{1, \dots, B\}$ is parameterized as a 2nd-order Dynamical Movement Primitive (DMP)~\cite{ijspeert2013dynamical}:
\begin{equation}
    \tau \dot{\mathbf{v}} = K (\mathbf{g}_\ell - \mathbf{x}) - D \mathbf{v} + (\mathbf{g}_\ell - \mathbf{x}_0) \odot \mathbf{f}_\ell(s), \quad \tau \dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{v}
\end{equation}
where $K=100$, $D=2\sqrt{K}$ enforces critical damping, $s \in (0, 1]$ is the canonical phase ($\tau \dot{s} = -\alpha_s s$), and $\mathbf{f}_\ell(s) = \frac{\sum_{p} \psi_p(s) \mathbf{w}_p}{\sum_{p} \psi_p(s)} s$ is a nonlinear forcing function with Gaussian bases $\psi_p(s)$ fit via linear ridge regression.

\textit{Numerical Stability \& Goal Anchoring:} Semi-implicit Euler integration requires $\Delta t < 2/\sqrt{K} = 0.2\,\text{s}$. Executing DMPs at the coarse baseline $5\,\text{fps}$ ($\Delta t = 0.2\,\text{s}$) causes numerical divergence; Flow2BT integrates primitives at $100\,\text{Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 10\,\text{ms}$), providing sub-$1.7\,\text{cm}$ tracking fidelity. To prevent receding waypoints from commanding unbounded accelerations ($> 45\,\text{m/s}^2$), the DMP goal is anchored upon activation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{g}_\ell = \mathbf{p}_i(t_{\text{start}}) + \mathbf{R}_i \Delta \mathbf{p}_\ell^{\text{cluster}}
\end{equation}
The leaf returns \texttt{RUNNING} while en route and \texttt{SUCCESS} when $\|\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{g}_\ell\| \le \epsilon$, prompting the root to re-tick and assign the next primitive.

\textbf{Behavior Tree Architecture \& $100\,\text{Hz}$ Execution:}
As detailed in \Fig{fig:overview} (right), condition guards and action leaves are assembled into a hierarchical control structure: Fallback nodes ($?$) handle priority preemption and branch recovery, while Sequence nodes ($\to$) chain condition checks $C_k(\mathbf{s})$ to action primitives $A_\ell$. Operating at $100\,\text{Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 10\,\text{ms}$ control period), the tree evaluates the control hierarchy in $< 0.05\,\text{ms}$, slashing decision latency from $2000\,\text{ms}$ down to $10\,\text{ms}$.

\textbf{Kinematic Acceleration Limits:}
To ensure dynamically bounded locomotion across $100\,\text{Hz}$ execution ticks ($\Delta t = 10\,\text{ms}$), nominal accelerations $\mathbf{u}_{\text{BT}} \in \mathbb{R}^2$ commanded by DMP leaves are bounded by physical motor limits ($\|\mathbf{u}_i\|_\infty \le a_{\max}$, $a_{\max} = 3.0\,\text{m/s}^2$) before semi-implicit Euler integration, preventing unrealistic velocity surges.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate closed-loop simulation on the real-world ETH crowd benchmark (\texttt{seq\_eth}, 406 total pedestrian agents)~\cite{bae2025crowdes}. Pedestrian agents spawn dynamically according to recorded empirical timestamps and initial coordinates, tracking NavMesh paths toward destination boundaries over a 1,000-frame simulation horizon ($200\,\text{s}$ at $5\,\text{fps}$). Following CrowdES~\cite{bae2025crowdes}, evaluation spans two tiers: \textbf{Scene-Level Realism} evaluates macroscopic spatial-temporal crowd distribution similarity against ground truth via Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) over spatial Density (Dens.) and dynamic active Population (Pop.) count; \textbf{Agent-Level Accuracy} evaluates microscopic trajectory quality, including Kinematics (Kinem., EMD over speed, acceleration, and duration), path alignment via Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), and Travel Time (Time) EMD.
We report the mean and standard deviation across 5 randomized trials (seeds 0--4) for Flow2BT, and compare against the released 20-trial CrowdES benchmark and the continuous Flow Policy teacher.

\subsection{Benchmark Performance Comparison}
As summarized in \Table{tab:benchmark} (left), the continuous Flow Policy teacher improves offline trajectory forecasting over CrowdES across all metrics (e.g., $\text{minFDE}_{20}$ drops $40.1\%$, $0.2727$ vs. $0.4550$). 

In closed-loop simulation (\Table{tab:benchmark}, right), Flow2BT approximately preserves macroscopic scene-level crowd distributions while operating at $100\,\text{Hz}$. When induced from ground-truth trajectories, Flow2BT closely matches CrowdES in spatial Density ($0.0182 \pm 0.0017$ vs. $0.0180$) and dynamic Population distribution ($0.185 \pm 0.018$ vs. $0.182$). When distilled from the generative Flow Policy teacher ($1024 \times 16$), Flow2BT demonstrates comparable structural density ($0.0229 \pm 0.0019$).

However, agent-level accuracy exhibits a noticeable trade-off relative to CrowdES: Kinematics EMD increases to $0.550 \pm 0.041$ (vs. $0.340$), DTW path deviation rises to $2.19 \pm 0.064$ (vs. $1.62$), and Travel Time EMD increases to $1.014 \pm 0.058$ (vs. $0.596$). This performance gap stems structurally from two factors: (1) the discrete compression of multimodal trajectories into $B=8$ unimodal DMP primitives, and (2) the finite-duration stop recovery mechanism, where agents resuming from stationary states enter slower attractor modes. While CrowdES relies on open-loop $2\,\text{s}$ chunking, Flow2BT provides continuous feedback at each $10\,\text{ms}$ tick, trading microscopic smoothness for reactive execution and symbolic auditability.

\subsection{Runtime Reactivity and Computational Efficiency}
\textbf{Reactivity Boost:} 
Existing chunked simulators such as CrowdES execute open-loop trajectories ($T_f = 2.0\,\text{s}$ at $0.5\,\text{Hz}$), leaving agents unresponsive between modal updates for up to $2000\,\text{ms}$. In contrast, Flow2BT updates control at $100\,\text{Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 10\,\text{ms}$), with the Behavior Tree hierarchy evaluating in $< 0.05\,\text{ms}$ per tick.

\textbf{Pathfinding Cache Efficiency:}
In addition, polyline projection caching slashes online $A^*$ NavMesh queries by $610\times$ ($164,980$ queries served with only 2 replans across the entire sequence), enabling high-throughput interactive crowd simulation.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented Flow2BT, a framework that distills continuous Flow Matching crowd policies into interpretable, reactive Behavior Trees with closed-form DMP action primitives. On the ETH benchmark, Flow2BT demonstrates approximate preservation of macroscopic spatial density and population distributions while enabling high-frequency $100\,\text{Hz}$ execution ($10\,\text{ms}$ control cycles) with human-auditable decision logic. Current evaluation is limited to the ETH pedestrian benchmark, and agent-level kinematics reflect a trade-off inherent in discrete leaf clustering. Future work will investigate richer feature sets and multi-modal sensory inputs to bridge the microscopic fidelity gap in complex 3D environments.
