Summary

The manuscript presents a clear and timely idea: distilling a continuous crowd-motion policy into a reactive Behavior Tree with DMP leaves. The pipeline, central equations, and main latency motivation are easy to follow, and Table 1 provides promising initial quantitative evidence. The main revision need is reproducibility and claim calibration: the paper does not yet specify the closed-loop simulation protocol sufficiently, the comparison is not statistically balanced, and the evidence for interpretability/reactive behavior is weaker than the strength of the corresponding claims. Address these points while keeping the paper concise.

sentence_edit

Strengths

  • The problem statement clearly motivates reactive, interpretable crowd simulation for autonomous-robot evaluation.
  • The proposed teacher-flow → hierarchical topology → SVM guards/DMP leaves pipeline is coherent and well illustrated in Fig. 1.
  • The paper gives formal definitions for the flow-matching objective, clustering distance, SVM condition nodes, and DMP leaves.
  • Table 1 provides a useful separation between offline teacher forecasting and closed-loop scene/agent metrics.
  • The manuscript has a concise, professional structure appropriate to a short conference paper.

Limitations

  • Closed-loop experimental conditions and safety-filter parameters are not sufficient for reproduction.
  • Unequal evaluation trial counts and absent uncertainty estimates limit the strength of the benchmark comparison.
  • The reported Flow2BT (Flow) agent-level errors are meaningfully worse than CrowdES but are not discussed as a trade-off.
  • Interpretability and reactive preemption are asserted but not directly demonstrated with an induced-tree example or controlled perturbation case.
  • The evaluation is limited to one ETH sequence, so broad generalization claims should be avoided.
